## Goal
The sixth rendering: Frank Lloyd Wright's house for Charles and Mabel Ennis (1924) at 2607 Glendower Avenue, Los Feliz. The largest
of his California "textile block" houses: a Maya-like temple of patterned concrete blocks along a ridge above Los Angeles, built block
by block on its real site, with the same viewer experience as the others. Its home-page card goes from "Coming soon" to live. It's
the house that is most literally made of blocks.

## Research (2026-11-09)
**Sources:**
- **HABS CA-1942** (Library of Congress record ca0227): one measured drawing sheet, the **west elevation** with a detail of the
  wrought-iron gates, plus data pages. There are no plans or sections.
- **OSM maps the house** as a Los Angeles County building footprint: way 426658118, built 1924, about 33 × 24 m, ground at about
  257 m. Its "height" tag (27 m) includes the ridge-top podium and retaining walls. The footprint isn't named or tagged with Wikidata
  (the house is Q578184), so placement would fit that way by its id.
- **Two smaller 1924 buildings** sit just below it: ways 426657426 and 426658131. They're probably the chauffeur's quarters and the
  garage below the motor court; to be confirmed against photographs.
- **Documented facts** (NRHP, Wikipedia, the Ennis House Foundation):
  - built 1924;
  - about 27,000 concrete blocks, each **16 × 16 in**, many patterned with a Maya-inspired motif, made from the site's own
    decomposed granite;
  - a long north loggia; teak ceilings; art-glass windows; a glass-mosaic fireplace (wisteria);
  - a swimming pool, added in 1940;
  - damaged in the 1994 earthquake and the 2005 storms, restored by 2011.

**As for the Schröder House (spec 019), there are no measured plans online.** The plan and heights would come from:
- OSM's footprint and HABS's one elevation (documented);
- published plan diagrams and photographs (marked **[assumed]**).

The 16-inch block module helps a lot: every wall, pier and terrace is a whole number of blocks, so the model can snap to that grid.

**The site:** a ridge in the Hollywood Hills, near the Stahl House's. The engine already handles the steep LA terrain, the
chaparral, and the city below with its lights at dusk (specs 003–005, 010b). The house sits on a massive podium of the same blocks,
with retaining walls down the slope.

**What the engine doesn't have yet:**
- **Placement by OSM id.** The data script's fit finds the house by its Wikidata tag, and this footprint has none: a small opt-in
  option, `fit.osmIds`.
- **Nothing else.** The textile blocks are drawn with the engine's own pieces (Q3).

## Proposed approach
- **Data:** `scripts/houses/ennis-house.mjs` with `fit.osmIds: [426658118]`.
- **The house** at `/ennis-house/`:
  - the house on its podium, the retaining walls and the motor court;
  - the pool and the smaller buildings below (Q4);
  - a thumbnail; its card set to live; its credits entry (spec 021).

**Build story** (roles per spec 012):
1. *The ridge* (**site**).
2. *The podium and retaining walls* (**foundation**).
3. *The terraces and the motor court* (**floor**).
4. *The textile-block walls and piers* (**structure**): course by course, the patterned bands rising.
5. *Teak, mosaic and furniture* (**interior**).
6. *Art glass and the loggia's windows* (**envelope**).
7. *The roofs and the parapets' upper bands* (**roof**).
8. *The pool, the chaparral and the gardens* (**landscape**).

About 4 minutes, like the larger houses.

  not regenerated.

## Out of scope
- The earthquake damage and the restoration (it's shown restored, as today).
- Exact block-by-block reproduction of each pattern variant.
- The interiors beyond the main rooms.

## Open questions
- [x] **Q1: Sources.** No measured plans are online: only HABS's one elevation, OSM's footprint and photographs. *Recommended: go
      ahead on those*, marking the plan [assumed] where it comes from published diagrams. If you have a book with plans (for example
      Sweeney's *Wright in Hollywood*, or the Ennis House Foundation's publications), I'd use it.
- [x] **Q2: Level of detail.** *Recommended: like the other houses.* That means the full exterior and podium; the main rooms (the
      loggia, the living room with the mosaic fireplace, the dining room); Wright's furniture where photographs show it; and the art
      glass. Each piece is marked [documented] or [assumed].
- [x] **Q3: The textile blocks.** A 16-inch block is 1⅓ studs, which doesn't divide into whole pieces, but three blocks make
      exactly 4 ft. *Recommended: keep the house at true size, and draw the blocks on that 4-ft, 3-block repeat.* Each 4 × 4 ft square
      of wall becomes a 4-stud panel in which patterned and plain pieces alternate as in the documented bands, the pattern shown as a
      darker inset tone. The rhythm of the blocks reads, and every dimension stays within ±1 ft. The alternative, one block to one
      stud, would make each block read clearly, but it shrinks the whole house by 25%.
- [x] **Q4: How much of the site?** *Recommended: the main house, its podium, the motor court, the pool and the chauffeur's
      quarters*, all on OSM footprints. The neighbours stay as the renderer draws them.
- [x] **Q5: Disclaimer.** *Recommended:* "Fan tribute in a generic toy-block style. Not affiliated with the Ennis House Foundation,
      the Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation or the LEGO Group." The credits page picks it up.
