## Goal
Walter Gropius's school building for the Bauhaus in Dessau (1925–26). It's a pinwheel of white wings:
- the three-storey **workshop wing** behind its glass curtain wall;
- a two-storey **bridge** over Bauhausstraße, which held the administration;
- the **north wing** of the vocational school;
- a low **link** with the entrance hall, the auditorium (Aula), the stage and the canteen;
- the five-storey **Prellerhaus** of student studios, with its small balconies.

It's built block by block on its real site between Gropiusallee and Bauhausstraße, with the same viewer, and takes a new live card
on the home page. On spec 054's list it's ★★★ (`fit: 3`).

The Haus am Horn (spec 063) is already on the site and is the Bauhaus's own model house. This one differs in ways the blocks can
show:
- **Size:** about 2,700 m² of footprint against the Haus am Horn's 161 m². It's a school, not a house: rooms for a hundred students
  and their workshops.
- **No front and no centre.** The Haus am Horn is a symmetric square round one tall room. The school is an asymmetric pinwheel that
  "must be walked around to be understood" (bauhaus-dessau.de). Its five parts differ in height, from one storey to six levels.
- **Glass.** The Haus am Horn has punched windows in rendered walls. Here the workshop wing's frame stands back from its edge, so
  three storeys of glass hang in front of it on three sides.
- **A bridge over a street,** which nothing else on the site has.
- **A flat town site** (Dessau's ground varies by about 1 m across the lot), against the Haus am Horn's slope above the Ilm.

## Research (2026-11-12)
**Status today:** owned and used by the **Stiftung Bauhaus Dessau** (founded 1994). The foundation is funded by the federal
government, the state of Saxony-Anhalt and the city of Dessau-Roßlau. The Hochschule Anhalt rents six ground-floor rooms in the
north wing, and the Prellerhaus's studios are let to visitors for overnight stays. Address: Gropiusallee 38, 06846 Dessau-Roßlau.
It's open daily as a museum, with exhibitions and guided tours.

It has been part of the **UNESCO World Heritage site** "Bauhaus and its Sites in Weimar, Dessau and Bernau" since 1996 (OSM:
`ref:whc=729-004`). It was listed in the GDR in 1964.

**History, as it bears on which state to build** (de.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, bauhaus-dessau.de):
- **1925–26:** commissioned in March 1925 and begun in September; topped out on 21 March 1926; opened on 4 December 1926.
- **1945:** an air raid in March 1945 damaged the curtain wall so badly that most of it was removed. Makeshift repairs followed:
  thin walls between the floors, and timber windows.
- **1976:** a restoration for the school's 50th anniversary. The curtain wall was rebuilt in **aluminium** rather than steel, for
  structural reasons, following a surviving original section.
- **1996–2006:** a full restoration costing €17 million, with the last works finished in 2009. The colours follow the foundation's
  research into the original scheme.
- **Since 2020:** the glass facade has been under long-term monitoring.

**Sources:**
- **de.wikipedia, *Bauhausgebäude Dessau*:**
  - the parts and their storeys:
    - the workshop wing: three storeys and a basement, with the curtain wall "from the ground floor to the third storey";
    - the north wing: three storeys and a basement;
    - the bridge: two storeys, on pillars over Bauhausstraße;
    - the link: given as one storey in one place and two in another, with ten window bays and four horizontal glazing bars on its
      north side;
    - the studio building (Prellerhaus): five storeys and a basement, with four window bays on its east front.
  - On the Prellerhaus's east front, from the first floor up, each window's right third is full height and opens onto a balcony.

## Open questions
- [x] **Q1: Which state?** *Recommended:* **the 1996–2006 restoration as it stands today.**
  - That means the 1976 aluminium curtain wall, which follows the 1926 lines, and the 2006 colours, which follow Scheper's scheme.
  - OSM's outlines and every photograph used are of today's building.
  - The alternative is December 1926. It looks almost the same, but nothing could be checked against it, and the original glass
    reflected more strongly.
- [x] **Q2: Large pieces** rather than blocks for the walls and slabs, as at Trellick. *Recommended: yes.*

- **The data script (Q3, A13–A14).** Two opt-in settings, both read only when a house sets them:
  - `fit.osmRelations: [ids]` fetches the relations (`out geom`) and assembles their outer rings into the house's outline, in
    place of the `way["building"]` match. The ring assembly the water areas already used (`key`, `assemble`) moved to module
    scope unchanged so both can use it.
  - `exportParts: { relations, ways }` writes the named OSM parts and ways into `lotTerrain.ts` as `LOT_PARTS`, so A2 and A4 can
    test each part, the four piers and Bauhausstraße's passage against OSM. The spec names only `fit.osmRelations`; this second
    setting is an addition for the tests (Bauhausstraße under the bridge is `highway=pedestrian`, which the road query leaves out,
    and the parts are `building:part`s it doesn't fetch).
  - **Byte-identical (A14):** every other house whose Overpass data is cached on this machine was regenerated with the changed
    script: 23 houses, and `git status` showed no change to any of their `neighborhood.bin` or `lotTerrain.ts`. Four (Barcelona
    Pavilion, Glass House, Habitat 67, Villa Savoye) have no cache here and weren't run. The unit test regenerates the Haus am
    Horn and compares bytes when its cache is present (skipped otherwise, since `scripts/.cache` isn't in git).
- **The fit:** relation 14318571 at **−68°** (searched −74…−62°), offset (166, 142), **IoU 0.979** against the parts' rectangles
  (the search takes about 6 minutes: 29,000 OSM cells × 25 rotations). Lot elevation 61.9 m. Lot `x −90…400, z −120…330` (490 ×
  450 ft). Two OSM buildings centre in the lot (the Hochschule's, east of the Prellerhaus and the north wing) and stand as plain
  neighbours.

- **How it's built:** `shell.ts` decides, for any cell, whether it's on the outside at that height (each part a plan and a
  height range, so walls appear where a neighbouring part stops, e.g. the entrance block above the link's roof) and what fills
  it (render, window, the curtain wall, the red door). The walls go up in storey bands of panel-long pieces; the envelope fills
  the openings. The curtain wall is full-height mullions every 4 studs, a transom every 9 plates and 3-stud panes, 1 stud in
  front of the slabs. Columns are 2 × 2 with 4 × 4 two-plate heads, 3 ft behind the glass, on a 16-stud grid in three rows.
  The entrance's "grey block" (A7) is drawn as a 14 × 14 stair tower at the corner by the bridge, its east face carrying the red
  door, the canopy and BAUHAUS (4 × 5-pixel letters of 2-plate tiles, read correctly from the east: checked in a render).
- **Interior:** benches on all three workshop floors (looms on the second upper floor), 5 rows of Aula seats facing the stage
  with 18 lamps hung from the roof under a silvered ceiling, 12 canteen tables with stools, partitions in the floors' colours in
  the north wing, the bridge and the Prellerhaus (seven rooms and a kitchen a floor), one studio furnished.
- **Trees:** OSM maps only 14 trees on the lot, all street trees along Gropiusallee (the spec's "30 within 120 m" reaches
  beyond the lot). They are drawn, plus 10 assumed limes on the lawns (none in front of the balconies' view), marked so in
  `trees.ts`. The tree nodes were taken once from the OSM API's map call and converted with the fit; they aren't produced by the
  data script.
