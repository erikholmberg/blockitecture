## Goal
Louis Kahn's Salk Institute for Biological Studies in La Jolla, California (1959–65). It's a research institute on a mesa above the
Pacific. Two mirror-image concrete laboratory wings face each other across a bare travertine plaza. A narrow channel of water runs
down the middle of the plaza toward the ocean, and at the equinoxes the sun sets along it. On the plaza side of each wing stand
detached **study towers**, where teak-clad studies look toward the sea. Behind them are the column-free laboratory floors. Each one
has a full-height **service floor** above it, and half of the building lies below the plaza, lit by sunken light wells. It's built
block by block on its real site, with the same viewer, and takes a new live card on the home page. On spec 054's list it's ★★★
(`fit: 3`).

The site already has several buildings that aren't houses (spec 059). This one differs from each in ways the blocks can show:
- **The Neue Nationalgalerie** (spec 058) is a steel roof over glass. **Unity Temple** (spec 062) is a poured-concrete cube on a
  street corner. **Trellick Tower** (spec 061) is a concrete slab in a city. **The Bauhaus Dessau** (spec 076) is a white pinwheel on a
  flat town site. **Taliesin West** (spec 077) is a low stone campus in the desert.
- **The building is symmetric about an open space,** not about a mass. Its main room is the plaza between the two wings, and Luis
  Barragán called that plaza "a facade to the sky".
- **Bare concrete with tie holes as the finish.** Unity Temple is concrete too, but the Salk's concrete is left as cast: V-grooved
  panel joints, and a regular grid of form-tie holes filled with lead plugs. Nothing is ground, filled or painted.
- **Half of it is below the ground.** It's six storeys, three of them below ground (salk.edu). The site's other buildings either
  stand on their ground or step down a slope. Here the lower floors are cut into a level mesa, with sunken courts beside them.
- **Floors that hold only services.** Each laboratory floor has an interstitial floor above it, the depth of the 9 ft Vierendeel
  trusses that span the lab.
- **Water as a line.** Fallingwater's stream, Habitat 67's river and the pools at Stahl and Taliesin West are areas of water. This one
  is a single line of water 206 ft long and under 2 ft wide.
- **The Pacific from a mesa.** Casa Malaparte, the Eames House and the Lovell Beach House are at or near the water. The Salk's plaza
  is about 370 ft above the sea and about 2,400 ft back from the shore, with the sea seen as a horizon at the plaza's end.

## Research (2026-11-13)
**Status today:** owned and used by the **Salk Institute for Biological Studies**, a non-profit research institute founded by Jonas
Salk. The City of San Diego gave it **27 acres** on the mesa (salk.edu; Docomomo). Scientists still work in Kahn's buildings, and the
institute runs architecture tours (salk.edu, "Visiting Salk"). Its recognitions:
- **a historical landmark in 1991** (Wikipedia). The designating body isn't named there; it's probably the City of San Diego, but
  that's not checked;
- the **AIA Twenty-five Year Award**, 1992 (Wikipedia);
- in 2006, the **California Historical Resources Commission** found the 27-acre site eligible for the National Register of Historic
  Places (Wikipedia). Wikidata's building item (Q128635401) carries NRHP reference **05001114**; whether it's listed or only found
  eligible wasn't checked against the National Park Service's database;
- the **Getty Conservation Institute** worked with the institute from 2013 to 2017 on the teak window walls. The repair cost
  $9.8 million and kept over two-thirds of the original teak (salk.edu; Docomomo Journal 58, 2018).

**Build story** (roles per spec 012):
1. *The mesa above the Pacific* (**site**): the lot's ground, the cut for the three lower storeys, and the light wells' pits.
2. *The retaining walls & the deep slabs* (**foundation**): the walls of the cut, the lowest floor's slab, and the light wells' walls.
3. *The lowest lab floor & the light wells' paving* (**floor**).
4. *Concrete, storey by storey* (**structure**):
   - the lab blocks' walls and the outer towers, rising lab floor, then truss floor, then lab floor, both wings together;
   - the Vierendeel trusses of each service floor;
   - the plaza's deck and the travertine plates, laid when the walls reach plaza level, with the benches;
   - the study towers and their bridges, the west offices and the east service towers.

   The tie holes are part of the walls.
5. *The labs, the pipes & the studies* (**interior**): the benches on the top lab floor, the ducts in the truss floors, the studies'
   desks and bookcases, and the library's shelves. This goes in inside the open concrete frame.
6. *Glass & teak* (**envelope**): the labs' glass along both sides and in the light wells, the teak window walls of the studies and
   the west offices, and the porches' railings.
7. *The flat roofs* (**roof**): the wings, the outer towers and the study towers. Nothing hangs from the roof.
8. *The river of life, the orange trees & the eucalyptus* (**landscape**): the channel's water, the basins and the cascade, the
   citrus grove, the lawns, the scrub and the eucalyptus.

**About 3½ minutes** (Q6). It's the site's biggest low building. The structure phase is the longest, about a minute, as the two
wings rise together.
