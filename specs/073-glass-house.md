## Goal
Philip Johnson's Glass House in New Canaan, Connecticut (1949): his own weekend house, a glass box on the edge of a bluff above
a pond, and the windowless Brick House facing it across a lawn court. It's built block by block on its real site, with the same
viewer, and takes a new live card on the home page. On spec 054's list it's ★★★.

The Farnsworth House (spec 022) is already on the site, and it's the same idea: one room in a steel-and-glass box. This one differs
in ways the blocks can show:
- **It sits on the ground,** on a brick platform 10 in above the lawn, where Farnsworth floats on stilts.
- **The frame is black,** set flush with the glass, and the roof stops at the corner columns. Farnsworth's is white, stands outside the
  glass, and has its slabs cantilevered past the columns.
- **A brick cylinder** (the bathroom, with the fireplace on its outer face) is the only thing that reaches the ceiling, and it rises
  through the roof. Farnsworth has a wooden core that stops short of the ceiling.
- **It's a pair:** the glass box faces a brick box with no windows on that side. Farnsworth stands alone.
- **The setting:** a lawn on a wooded Connecticut ridge, with the ground falling about 60 ft to a pond, where Farnsworth has a flat
  river meadow.

## Research (2026-11-12)
**Status today:** owned by the National Trust for Historic Preservation, which opened it to visitors in April 2007; tours run from
a visitor centre in town (199 Elm Street). The district became a National Historic Landmark on 18 February 1997 (NRHP 97000341).
The Brick House was restored and reopened in May 2024 and is now on every tour.

**No HABS record.** The Library of Congress's HABS/HAER/HALS collection has no survey of the Glass House (searched "New Canaan":
only the Merritt Parkway, the station and three houses). So there are no measured drawings like Farnsworth's HABS IL-1105. The model
rests on the published dimensions, OSM's outlines, a published plan of the Brick House and photographs.

**Sources:**
- **The National Historic Landmark nomination** (NPS, 1997; NPGallery asset 1634a249…, public domain). This is the best source:
  - **The Glass House:** "32 feet by 56 feet in plan, with a height of 10½ feet".
    - The walls are floor-to-ceiling plate glass between black-painted steel piers, set with angle brackets on stock H-beams.
    - The foundation is a brick platform. The floor is brick in herringbone, and the cylinder is the same glazed brick, "deep reds
      and browns with lighter-colored flecks".
    - The cylinder is off-centre, with the fireplace on one side and the bathroom door on the other, and it rises through the flat roof.
    - The kitchen is one "long line of 42″ high cabinets" (sink, two refrigerators, stove, a liquor closet opening to the living
      area). It has six spotlights above it.
    - Low cabinets and bookshelves divide the rest of the room.
  - **The lawn:** crushed-stone paths to the Brick House, and a low light-grey granite curb round the lawn's outer edge. The entrance
    is a full-height glass door centred on the **east** front, up low granite steps.
  - **The Brick House** (the "Guest House"), "about 80 feet to the southeast": **18 × 52 ft**, in the same brick, laid in Flemish
    bond. It has a single black door on the **west** front (facing the Glass House) and three large round windows on the **east**
    (rear) front. A plain sheet-metal frieze is its only cornice, under a flat roof.
  - **The swimming pool** (1955): round, 32 ft across, north of the two houses, with a light-grey granite deck.
  - **The Pavilion** (1962): precast concrete open colonnades in the pond, "about 32 feet square overall", a four-armed plan over
    water channels, at about two-thirds of normal scale.
  - **The setting:** the land slopes gently from Ponus Ridge Road to a bluff, then "descends much more steeply to a small pond and
    wooded area" on the west.
  - **Later buildings, with sizes:** the Painting Gallery (1965), the Sculpture Gallery (1970), the Study (1980; 15 × 20 ft with a
    12 ft cylinder), the Ghost House (1984), the Lincoln Kirstein Tower (1985; 36 ft, west of the pond) and Da Monsta (1995).
- **theglasshouse.org** (the National Trust's site): the pages for the Glass House, the Brick House, the Pavilion and the landscape.
  - **The Glass House:**
    - "55 feet long, 33 feet wide, 1,815 square feet". This disagrees with the nomination's 56 × 32 ft (see Q2).
    - **A centred glass door in each of the four walls.**
    - The "rooms": kitchen, dining, living, bedroom, hearth, bathroom, entrance.
    - Walnut-veneered storage cabinets between the bedroom and the living room, and a small desk in the bedroom.
    - Most of the furniture came from Johnson's 1930 New York apartment, designed by Mies van der Rohe. Mies designed the daybed
      for Johnson.
    - A rug marks the living room, with seating round a low table.
    - *The Burial of Phocion* (attributed to Poussin) faces Elie Nadelman's *Two Circus Women*.
    - The house stands on "a knoll with a large oak tree", which Johnson called central to the design.
  - **The Brick House:** completed a few months before the Glass House, with a grassy court linking the two. Its only windows,
    besides skylights, are the round ones at the rear. The 1953 remodelling gave it a bedroom with low white plaster vaults (after
    Soane's breakfast room), a freestanding plaster canopy "ten by thirty feet", a skylit corridor and a reading room.
  - **The Pavilion:** prefabricated concrete arches and roof sections on a poured base, "at the edge of a man-made pond located at
    the bottom of a hill below the Glass House"; it once had a 20 ft fountain jet.
- **Wikipedia** (Glass House (New Canaan, Connecticut); wikidata Q1506237): 56 × 32 × 10.5 ft; the brick floor 10 in above the
  ground; charcoal-painted steel; the Rippowam River valley to the west. The original 11-acre tract has grown to about 49 acres.
- **ArchDaily, *AD Classics: The Glass House*** (2010): it repeats the nomination's figures. Its gallery has plans of the **Brick

## Proposed approach
- **Data:** `scripts/houses/glass-house.mjs`, fitted by OSM's way 364907964 (`wikidata: 'Q1506237'`), with a rotation search around
  its 357° bearing. y = 0 is pinned to the brick floor, 10 in above the terrain's reading at the house (`lotElevationM`, as for
  Farnsworth). The pond is drawn with the `waterAreas` option (spec 022), and the real ground is kept, bluff and all.
- **The Glass House** at `/glass-house/`:
  - the brick platform and the herringbone floor, 10 in above the lawn;
  - the black steel: corner piers and a pier at each joint of the glass, the fascia, and the rail;
  - the glass, with a door centred in each of the four walls;
  - the brick cylinder, from the floor up through the roof, with the fireplace on its outer face and the bathroom inside;
  - the flat roof, ending flush at the corner piers, and the six kitchen spotlights hung from it;
  - inside, as blocky stand-ins:
    - the 42″ walnut kitchen unit;
    - the tall walnut storage wall and the bed behind it, with the desk;
    - the living area's rug, Barcelona chairs, stool, daybed and glass table;
    - the Poussin on its easel and the Nadelman on a plinth;
    - a dining table and chairs **[assumed]**.
- **The Brick House** (if Q1 says yes): 18 × 52 ft of brick, with the black door on the west and three round windows on the east.
  Inside go the corridor, the bathroom, the reading room and the vaulted bedroom with its plaster canopy, from ArchDaily's plan. The
  canopy and vaults are block stand-ins.
- **The landscape:**
  - the lawn and the gravel court between the houses, with the granite curb and the granite entrance steps;
  - the big oak on the knoll, and the trees round the lawn and down the bluff;
  - the stone wall from OSM;
  - the pool and its granite deck, and the pond with the Pavilion (if Q4 says yes).
- **The usual extras:** a thumbnail; a live card with its credits (the NHL nomination, OSM, AWS terrain tiles, the Library of
  Congress photographs); its definition added to `DEFINITIONS` in `tests/buildOrder.test.ts`.

**Build story** (roles per spec 012):
1. *The knoll above the pond* (**site**): the lot's ground, the bluff, the pond's basin.
2. *The brick platforms* (**foundation**): both houses' bases, the Glass House's 10 in above the lawn.
3. *The herringbone brick floors* (**floor**).
4. *The black steel, the brick cylinder & the Brick House's walls* (**structure**):
   - the piers and the fascia stand alone on the platform;
   - the cylinder rises through where the roof will be;
   - the Brick House's load-bearing walls go up, as the Haus am Horn's did (spec 063).
5. *The kitchen, the walnut cabinets & Mies's furniture* (**interior**): this goes in inside the open frame, along with the Brick
   House's rooms and its plaster canopy.
6. *The glass, the four doors & the rail* (**envelope**). The Brick House's black door and its three round windows go in too.
7. *The flat roofs* (**roof**). The six spotlights hang from the Glass House's roof (`b.hanging(P.ROOF, …)`).
8. *The oak, the trees, the court, the pool, the pond & the Pavilion* (**landscape**). The Pavilion stands on its own base in the
   pond, so it touches something already built.

**About 2½ minutes.** The Glass House alone is smaller than Farnsworth, and the Brick House and the Pavilion bring it back up to
about that size.
