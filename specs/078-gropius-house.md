## Goal
Walter Gropius's own house in Lincoln, Massachusetts (1938), designed with Marcel Breuer. It was his first building in America.
It's a white two-storey box of vertical wood siding on a levelled hilltop above an apple orchard. Set against that plain box are a
few pieces: an entrance canopy angled off the front on two steel posts, a wall of glass block, an outdoor spiral stair up to a
deck, a screened porch reaching into the garden, and two trellises. It's built block by block on its real site, with the same
viewer, and takes a new live card on the home page. On the dev shortlist it's ★★★ (`fit: 3`).

Two Bauhaus buildings are already on the site, the Haus am Horn (spec 063) and Gropius's own Bauhaus Dessau (spec 076). This one
differs from both in ways the blocks can show:
- **Wood, not render.** Both German buildings are rendered masonry and concrete. This is a wood frame clad in **white-painted
  vertical boards**, a New England material laid in a Bauhaus way. It's the site's first house whose walls are siding.
- **The front is a collage.** The Haus am Horn is a symmetric square. The Bauhaus is a pinwheel of whole wings. This house is
  one plain rectangle with small pieces fixed to it: the canopy at 30°, the glass-block wall, the spiral stair, the trellises and
  the porch.
- **Glass block.** Nothing on the site has a wall of glass block yet. Here there are two: one outside at the entrance, and one
  inside between the study and the dining room.
- **An outdoor spiral stair.** The Sonneveld House (spec 060) has one down to its garden. This one climbs a full storey on the
  house's front to a deck cut out of the upper floor.
- **A hilltop in New England woods,** with an orchard on the slope below. The Haus am Horn sits on a town slope and the Bauhaus
  on flat ground. Like the Glass House (spec 073), this one gets the `forest` scenery, but on a rounded hilltop, not a bluff.

## Research (2026-11-13)
**Status today:** owned by **Historic New England** (the Society for the Preservation of New England Antiquities, SPNEA,
until 2004). Ise Gropius gave it to SPNEA. Wikipedia dates the gift to 1974–75; the NHL nomination says "deeded … in 1980". She
lived there until shortly before her death in 1983. The house opened as a museum on 1 June 1985 (Wikipedia; the nomination says
1984), with guided tours sold in advance. The museum shows the house as it was in **Gropius's last years, 1965–69**. In
2025–26 Historic New England ran a design competition for a new restroom and visitor building by the garage; Isabel Strauss's
entry won.

Its recognitions:
- a contributing building in the **Woods End Road Historic District**, on the National Register since 8 July 1988, together with
  four 1938–39 houses to the west by Gropius, Breuer and Walter Bogner;
- a **National Historic Landmark** since 16 May 2000 (NRIS **00000709**);
- MassGIS protected open space: "protected: perpetuity", owner SPNEA (OSM way 29830249, from MassGIS).

## Open questions
- [x] **Q1: Which state?** *Recommended: Gropius's last years, about 1965–69,* the period the museum interprets. That means:

  - the Japanese garden south of the porch (1957);
  - the orchard as a 1960s grid;
  - the furniture as the house shows it today.

  The HABS photographs (1988) and today's house both match this. The *alternative* is 1938 as built: a white deck wall, the
  perennial border, and the original 90-tree orchard. No photographs were examined for that state, so more of it would be
  [assumed].
- [x] **Q2: Disclaimer.** *Recommended:* "Fan tribute. Not affiliated with Historic New England." Historic New England owns the
  house, runs the tours and holds its archive. One body to name is enough, as for Taliesin West. The *alternative* adds "or the
  Gropius House", as the Glass House's names the house itself.
- [x] **Q3: Timing.** *Recommended: about 2½ minutes,* like the Glass House (2:29): a small house on a large wooded lot. The
  *alternative* is about 2 minutes with a smaller lot (Q5's smaller option).
- [x] **Q4: The room layout without a plan.** No measured plan was found online. HABS has no drawings, and the full Conservation
  Management Plan didn't download.
  - *Recommended:* if you have a plan (Ise Gropius's *History of the Gropius House*, 1977, Giedion's *Walter Gropius*, or the
    competition website's drawings), a scan lets me scale every partition, as for the Haus am Horn.
  - Failing that, I place the rooms from the nomination's descriptions and the HABS interior photographs, and mark them
    **[assumed]**:
    - the living room across the west end, 14.4 ft deep;
    - the study east of it on the north side, with the dining room south of the study;

    glass-block partition would be lost.
