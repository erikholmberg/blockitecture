## Goal
Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin West in Scottsdale, Arizona (from 1937): his winter home, studio and school, which he and his apprentices
built by hand on the desert slope below the McDowell Mountains. Walls of desert stone set in concrete lean inward under red-painted
redwood beams and white canvas roofs. It runs as one long spine of rooms: the drafting studio, the kitchen and dining rooms, the
Garden Room and Wright's quarters. In front of the spine, a triangular terrace points southwest like a ship's prow, with a
triangular pool. It's built block by block on its real site, with the same viewer, and takes a new live card on the home page. On
spec 054's list it's ★★.

The site has three Wright buildings already. This one differs from each in ways the blocks can show:
- **Fallingwater** (spec 017) is smooth concrete trays cantilevered over a stream in a forest. **Ennis** (spec 025) is a solid of
  patterned textile blocks on a Los Angeles ridge. **Unity Temple** (spec 062) is a poured-concrete cube on a street corner. All
  three have flat roofs and walls in one material.
- **This is Wright's first building on the site with sloped roofs.** The roofs are single shed slopes of about 15°, carried on a row
  of exposed beams that stick out past the roof's edge.
- **The walls are a mix of colours:** rough stones of many colours set in grey-buff concrete, with the walls leaning inward. On the
  other Wright buildings, each wall is one colour.
- **It's a campus, not one building.** It's a long spine of rooms on a grid, with wings and terraces breaking off it at 45°.
- **The desert.** It's the site's second desert building after the Kaufmann Desert House (spec 033). It reuses that spec's
  `desert` palette, on a foothill slope rather than a flat valley floor.

## Research (2026-11-12)
**Status today:** the complex is owned by the **Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation**, which is based there (its address is Taliesin
West). The Foundation runs tours year-round, with over 100,000 visitors a year. Its recognitions:
- listed on the National Register of Historic Places, 12 February 1974 (NRIS 74000457);
- a **National Historic Landmark** since 20 May 1982;
- part of the **UNESCO World Heritage** listing "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright" since 2019 (ref. 1496-007,
  criterion ii), together with Fallingwater and Unity Temple, which are already on the site.

The School of Architecture left in 2020. Wikipedia says the Foundation holds a trademark on the name "Taliesin West" (not checked
against the USPTO).

**Sources:**
- **Wikipedia** (*Taliesin West*; wikidata **Q1208310**):
  - the dates: site bought December 1937, work from 1938, the compound mostly complete by 1940–41, the music pavilion 1957;
  - "desert masonry": local rock set in wooden formwork and filled with concrete, the walls leaning inward about 15°;
  - redwood frames, later strengthened with Cherokee-red steel;
  - canvas roof panels on redwood frames, pitched about 15°. They were replaced over time with fibreglass, and with acrylic in 1998.
  - **the drafting studio** about 100 ft long, its ceiling sloping from 6 to 13 ft;
  - **the Garden Room** about 56 × 34 ft, facing south;
  - Wright's quarters an L-shaped wing of 114 × 20 ft; the dining room 40 × 28 ft;
  - a 16 × 16 ft planning module, with the wings meeting at 45°;
  - the **prow-shaped Sunset Terrace** on the main building's western and southern sides. At its northern tip, the dining room
    and drafting studio wrap round **Indian Rock Terrace**, a stepped pyramid topped by a petroglyph boulder;
  - **the triangular pool** in front of Indian Rock Terrace, fed from the original well and meant as a fire reservoir. The rest
    of Sunset Terrace is lawn;
  - the Kiva, partly underground, with a concrete roof; a citrus grove north-east of the main building;
  - about 1,600 ft above sea level, in a gully at the foot of the McDowell Mountains, looking south over the valley.
- **The Historic American Buildings Survey, HABS AZ-218** (Library of Congress; "no known restrictions"). The measured drawings
  are by University of Texas at San Antonio students for the Charles E. Peterson Prize, sponsored by the Foundation. The record
  for the whole complex (AZ-218, LOC az0508) has **no drawings**: the Foundation "would not release the copyright to the 31
  drawings" made under a Save America's Treasures grant, so they sit unprocessed in the field notes. Seven building records
  (AZ-218-A to -G) do have drawings; the two read for this spec:
  - **AZ-218-D, the Drafting Studio** (2017–19; 12 sheets, 9 data pages). This is the best source.
    - About **30′-0″ × 108′-0″**, 3,240 sq ft, "rotated approximately 45 degrees from true north", its long axis NW–SE.
    - **Twelve even bays** set by the cross beams. These are **flitch beams**: redwood either side of a steel web. There's no
      ridge beam.
    - On the north-east side, five desert-masonry piers carry the beams (the plan's dimensions put a pier about every 16 ft,
      two bays). The two south-eastern bays there are filled with 4′-6″ desert-masonry walls.
    - The shed roof "falling northeast to southwest at 15 degrees". Today it's thick opaque acrylic boxes over foam, with
      wood-framed canvas panels forming the ceiling.
    - The south-west front: a desert-masonry base "in two battered courses", with steel-framed window bays cantilevered over it.
      The north-west end is the **Vault**, the first desert-masonry building on the site, with a shed roof at the studio's slope.
      Beside it are a small walled patio and a three-walled masonry office bay.
    - On the north-east side, a **pergola** of wood and steel, with four pairs of doors opening onto it. A gold-and-white
      chequered brise-soleil there copies the old canvas shades.
    - A fireplace shared with the kitchen at the south-east end. Its chimney rises **16 ft** from the foundation.
    - The east eave projects **2′-1″**. The dentils on the fascias are 2″ × 2″, 4″ apart.
    - The floor is bare concrete painted **Cherokee Red**, with a flagstone area and a stepped fire pit at the south-east end.
    - Wright's drafting tables and benches, in Cherokee Red; carved "icicle" pendant lamps hung across the studio.
    - Its changes: open-air in 1938, with lapped redwood boards and canvas; all canvas flaps in 1948; glazing from the late 1940s;
      transparent fibreglass in 1964; acrylic in 1998.
  - **AZ-218-G, the Garden Room & Dining Cove** (2022–23; 7 sheets, 11 data pages):
    - the Garden Room about **53′-5″ × 29′-6″**, the Dining Cove **23′-4″ × 15′-2″**, connected by a glass door to the north;
    - built in 1940;
    - "seven structural fins" separating panels in a roof that slopes one way. The roof fin is 26′-2⅝″ long, and the ceiling
      rises from close to 8 ft to around 11 ft;
    - a separate roof over the south end, sloping south, where a big desert-masonry fireplace stands;
    - flat roofs over the entrance (with a pentagon skylight) and the Dining Cove;
    - a pivot door of 4′ × 6′-3″, and trapezoidal doors fitted to the masonry;
    - glass on the north and east sides, with built-in benches along the west wall;
    - the floor was concrete with a rose shag carpet, now white;
    - its plan places Sunset Terrace south of the room and labels "The Prow" at its corner, with a diagonal wall.
  - **The other five records** have drawings that weren't read for this spec: AZ-218-A (the Original Dining Room, 7 sheets), -B
    (the Cabaret, 1950; 11 sheets), -C (the Formal Office, 6 sheets), -E (the Dining Room, formerly the loggia; 9 sheets) and -F

  - *Alternative, smaller:* the studio, the Garden Room and the prow only. That's cheaper, but it loses the spine that makes it a
    campus.
- [x] **Q2: Which state?** *Recommended: as it stands today* (the studio's 1998 roof, the glazed walls, the Garden Room as
  restored in 2021). OSM, the HABS drawings (2017–23) and the photographs all show this state. The *alternative* is about 1959,
  at Wright's death: canvas roofs, with some walls still open or canvas-flapped. No measured drawing shows it, so most of it would
  be [assumed].
- [x] **Q3: Canvas roofs or today's panels?** Today, the studio's outer layer is opaque white acrylic boxes, with wood-framed
  canvas panels as the ceiling beneath. The Garden Room has fibreglass panels between its fins. Seen from outside, both read as
  white panels between red beams, as the canvas once did. *Recommended: one warm "canvas" white for every sloped panel, inside and
  out, with no new material.* It reads as canvas from inside and as today's white panels from outside, and it keeps the
  Foundation's "canvas roofs" story. The *alternative* is a cooler acrylic white outside, which is more literal but makes the
  ceiling a different colour from the roof.
- [x] **Q4: Disclaimer.** *Recommended:* "Fan tribute. Not affiliated with the Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation." The Foundation owns
  the site, is based there and runs the tours. Wikipedia says it holds a trademark on the name "Taliesin West". The name is used
  only to say which building this is, as Fallingwater's name is. One body to name is enough, unlike Unity Temple, which also named
  its congregation and the Frank Lloyd Wright Trust.
- [x] **Q5: Which size for the Garden Room?** HABS AZ-218-G gives 53′-5″ × 29′-6″, Wikipedia about 56 × 34 ft, and OSM's trace
  33 × 48 ft. OSM's Dining Cove is also far larger than HABS's. *Recommended: HABS's*, since it's measured. The rooms are placed
  inside OSM's two ways together (A4), not on either alone.
- [x] **Q6: Unidentified buildings.** OSM's ways 1498902782 and 1498897833, at the spine's south-east end, aren't named, and
  nothing found here identifies them. *Recommended: leave them out of the lot as OSM massing*, and end the lot after Wright's
  quarters (way 1498902778). If you know them to be Wright's bedroom wing or the guest deck, I'd model them as exteriors like the
  quarters.
- [x] **Q7: Timing.** *Recommended: about 3 minutes*, like Farnsworth and the Kaufmann Desert House. The spine is long but one
  storey.
